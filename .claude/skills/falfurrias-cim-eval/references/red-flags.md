# Red Flags — Auto-Disqualify and Caution Signals

Falfurrias is theme-first and disciplined. Many CIMs that look fine on paper fail their internal screen for reasons that won't show up in DCF math. Use this checklist after audited extraction; surface any hit in the teaser screen and IC memo.

Three categories: **Auto-pass** (almost always decline), **Strong caution** (proceed only with explicit thesis match), **Soft caution** (note in memo).

## Auto-pass (strong default decline)

- **Outside core sectors with no clear theme bridge.** If no entry in `firm-profile.md`'s active themes maps to the target, decline unless the deal champion can argue a new theme. (Stretch beyond the bench = high risk for Falfurrias's model.)
- **Sub-$1M EBITDA or sub-$5M ARR.** Below the FGP floor.
- **Outside-US primary geography.** No public evidence of non-US platforms.
- **Restricted categories.** Cannabis, gambling, firearms, payday lending, adult content.
- **Pure consumer-direct with no B2B anchor.** Marketing-spend dependent businesses.
- **Pre-revenue or pre-EBITDA tech.** Falfurrias does growth, not venture.
- **Asset-heavy with no recurring layer.** Pure project-revenue construction, fab-only manufacturing without aftermarket/services.
- **Hostile process / management opposes sale.** Falfurrias does partnership deals.

## Strong caution (proceed only with explicit thesis match)

- **Customer concentration top-1 >25%, top-3 >50%** without multi-year contracts.
- **Single supplier risk** (one critical input from a sole source).
- **Adjusted EBITDA addbacks >20% of reported EBITDA**, especially "one-time" items that recur.
- **Recurring revenue overstated.** The CIM calls "re-occurring" repeat purchases "recurring." Margin compression at renewal is a tell.
- **Founder unwilling to roll equity** or planning immediate departure with no succession.
- **Bullish projections >2x trailing growth** without contracted backlog.
- **TAM single-sourced and unfounded** (banker estimate, no third-party support).
- **CEO already paid out** in a prior recap, low alignment going forward.
- **Working capital release driving recent FCF improvement** — i.e. inventory liquidation that isn't sustainable.
- **Heavy goodwill from past acquisitions** with integration not visible operationally.
- **Heavy CAPEX in projection** (>5% of revenue) without explained ROIC.

## Soft caution (note in memo, don't decline)

- **First-time PE process.** Banker presentation may be raw; price expectations may be misaligned.
- **Regional concentration** (>60% revenue from one state/region) — flag whether it's defensible or limiting.
- **Average customer tenure <3 years** in a category where 5+ is normal.
- **Union exposure** in a non-unionized peer set.
- **Pending litigation** — not auto-dq but always flag the exposure quantum.
- **Cybersecurity gap** disclosed in CIM — relevant for govt-services and FI-serving businesses.
- **Owner-operator concentration** — CEO is the rainmaker on customer relationships.
- **Carveout from larger parent** — TSA risk, standalone cost basis unclear.
- **Non-GAAP-only financials.** Falfurrias's DD will rebuild on GAAP.
- **No backlog disclosure** when the business is project-based.
- **Customer churn definition lawyered** (e.g. "logo churn excluding voluntary terminations").

## Where to surface in the outputs

- **Teaser screen:** any auto-pass = recommend PASS with single-sentence reason.
- **IC memo:** dedicate a "Risks & Red Flags" section with strong cautions in a table; soft cautions in a callout. Use the colored-callout pattern from the AGCO-style guide.

## Calibration notes (Falfurrias-specific)

- Customer concentration is *more* concerning at FCP scale than FGP — FCP is buying durability; FGP is buying growth where concentration can dilute over time.
- Heavy capex is a stronger negative for FGP (growth fund) than for FCP industrial-tech, which has higher tolerance.
- Founder rollover < 20% should trigger a conversation; <10% is usually a deal-killer for the operating-partner model to work.
- Sector tailwind without a sourceable third-party data point is a dealbreaker on the "Industry First" framing — the Strategy & Market Insights team will not sign off on a thesis without it.
